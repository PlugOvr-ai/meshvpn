meshvpn enterprise

The same program with access control for shared benches, labs and clusters.

Developer experience enterprise

Feels like your own workstation. Keeps the cluster busy.

Developers want one machine with their files, their tools and sudo. A shared cluster wants every GPU working around the clock. With jobs and homes, you get both: queued jobs fill every idle hour, step aside when someone books a node, and continue elsewhere with everything where it was.

One name, wherever it runs

ssh train42.mesh reaches your job's machine today, tomorrow and after it moved. No IP addresses, no "which node was it again".

Your home comes along

Code, checkpoints, logs and dotfiles are copied to the next machine before your job continues, only what changed. Several homes for different projects, each with its own rules.

Resume, don't restart

When a node is needed elsewhere, your job gets a stop request, saves its checkpoint and continues from it later. Hours of training aren't lost.

No warnings, no friction

Host certificates make ssh trust every node, also after a move. sudo while the machine is yours. VS Code Remote, scp and rsync work as on your desk.

How a booking works

From request to sudo, and back to locked.

Book

An admin, a user in the portal or an agent through the API books devices for a time. Exclusive bookings never overlap; shared ones share with other shared ones.

Work

During the booking the device has an account for the user, with sudo if the rule allows it. The gateway lets the user's key through to the booked devices, and to nothing else.

Release

When the time is up the account is locked, sudo is removed and the user's processes stop, including open sessions. The audit log keeps who had which device when.

Under the hood

Every node is equal. Traffic takes the shortest path.

Nodes sign their own records and gossip them. Links are Noise-encrypted, every packet is also encrypted end to end, and relays can neither read nor forge what they forward.
