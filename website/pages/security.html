Security

What protects your network, and where the limits are.

meshvpn carries your traffic and decides who gets onto your machines. Here is how it does that, what it doesn't do, and how to tell us about a problem.

Report a vulnerability

Please don't open a public issue. Write to security@plugovr.ai or use GitHub's private reporting. Tell us the version, the platform, what an attacker needs and what they gain.

We don't take legal action against research in good faith: on your own systems or with permission, without accessing other people's data beyond what shows the problem, and with reasonable time before disclosure. Our policy: SECURITY.md, and security.txt.

How it works

PieceDesign
IdentityEvery node has an ed25519 key (its id) and an X25519 key. Names and addresses are derived from or bound to that identity.
MembershipLinks use Noise XXpsk3 with the network key as the pre-shared key: without it, the handshake fails. Invites carry a ticket signed by an admin, bound to the first node that uses it.
EncryptionHop-by-hop Noise encryption, plus end-to-end XChaCha20-Poly1305 on every packet. Relays can't read or forge traffic, and the inner source address must match the sender.
RecordsEvery node signs its own record; nodes only accept records with valid signatures and newer sequence numbers.
BanningA ban rotates the network key and sends the new key to every other member, sealed for each one. The banned node can't rejoin with what it knows.
SSHKeys allowed through meshvpn ssh are limited to the node's mesh address, which can't be spoofed on the mesh. Host keys are published, so there are no "are you sure?" prompts to click through.
Policy enterpriseSigned by admins and checked on every node. Devices create accounts only for current bookings and only accept keys from the gateway. Gateways forward each key only to its booked devices.
Portal enterprisePasswords hashed with Argon2id, mandatory TOTP second factor, API keys stored as hashes.
UpdatesNodes never update themselves. meshvpn update and the installers check SHA-256 checksums of every download.

Limits you should know

Members are trusted

Nodes in a network can reach each other's services. Treat invite codes like passwords until they're used or expire, and keep firewalls on services that shouldn't be reachable from the mesh.

Checksums, not signatures

Checksums protect against broken downloads, not against a compromised GitHub account.

End-to-end layer

The end-to-end layer has no replay protection or forward secrecy. The hop-by-hop Noise links have both.

A tool, not your whole security

meshvpn is one part of your security measures. Install updates, protect keys and invites, give access only as needed, and review the audit log.
